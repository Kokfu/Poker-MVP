# Phase 4I — Decision sensitivity and exploitation validation

**Verdict.** With the corrected model, enabling adaptation changed 9 of 16 seeded recommendations and changed at least 5 percentage points of action probability in 10 spots. The Phase 4H correction itself changed 0 of 16 recommendations and no more than 0.59 percentage points of action probability, even where villain-range TV reached 39.39 points. The correction did not meet the paired benchmark trigger. This phase establishes decision sensitivity to adaptation and leaves its incremental playing-strength effect unresolved.

Phase 4H established the mechanical correctness of the opening-lock coefficient. Calibration of the inferred combo distributions against true opponent ranges remains unmeasured.

## Decision pipeline

| Stage | What reaches the decision | Limits and display behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observation and replay | The Coach advice route validates the entered spot, replays legal public actions through `HandEngine` and stops at Hero's decision. It rebuilds a named opponent's snapshot from completed SQLite records. `OpponentModel` counts public actions and opportunities. `ShowdownModel` separately observes revealed hands at completed showdowns. With no recorded hands the Coach uses the base solver. The type label and VPIP describe the opponent. Neither selects a solver policy. |
| Preflop | Both public ranges start uniform. The solver selects the nearest effective depth from 15/30/60/100/200 bb and maps observed actions to the chart. Without a usable lock it reads the stored policy. With one it re-solves the 169-class game for 250 iterations and caches by depth, opponent seat and rounded locks. Each replayed action multiplies its actor's reach by the action likelihood for each combo. The corrected SB-root coefficient changes that likelihood for the BB facing an open and can change the entire coupled chart re-solve. [Solver bot](backend/solver/bot.py#L277) · [Chart re-solve](backend/solver/bot.py#L435) · [Lock selection](backend/solver/exploit.py#L99) | The unopened-SB lock is absent when Hero is the button. Those spots can still adapt to the villain's BB policies. VPIP, the label and raw sizing statistics are unused by the default chart solve. The chart stores frequencies and supplies no per-action EV. |
| Postflop | Replay masks board blockers, prunes weights at 0.5% of the maximum, keeps Hero's combo and solves each street with the two public reaches and compatible-combo equity matrices. Observed actions update reach from the solver's average policy. On the current street an off-tree size first inherits the closest abstract action's range update, then triggers a solve from the actual pot/stack geometry. The CFR traversal also fits current and final opponent policies to the local lock groups, changing the response incentives. [Postflop replay and solve](backend/solver/bot.py#L318) · [DCFR policy](backend/solver/cfr.py#L224) | Default Coach locks are partially pooled across streets. It uses opening bet and facing fold/call/raise reads with size-agnostic groups. The 50%/100% pot bet menu, pot raise and all-in compress actions. Standard budgets are 60/70/80 iterations for flop/turn/river. Flop uses 30 sampled runouts. Turn and river enumerate remaining chance exactly. Flop and turn showdown leaves omit later betting. The deviation gate and size-conditioned experiment are off in Coach. |
| Recommendation and explanation | The bot samples the Hero combo's full-precision mix from the spot's stable hash seed, then projects the selected action to the engine's authoritative legal target. The Coach aggregates concretized preflop labels for display. The separate range report blocks Hero's cards, computes effective combos and equity, and never feeds a scalar back into the solver. The weighted ranges and equity matrices already entered the decision upstream. [Recommendation and projection](backend/solver/bot.py#L499) · [Explanation](backend/coach/explain.py) | A frequency shift may leave the same sampled action. Equal concrete actions can arise from different abstract actions. `adjusted` detects locks in the returned solve, so it can be true with identical advice and false if adaptation arrived through prior ranges only. Standard Coach postflop `unlocked_action_differs` is unset because it does not run a local unlocked comparison. On supported solver exceptions Expert advice is substituted and counted separately from engine substitutions. |

## Exploitation quality

- A lock sets a target for action groups such as fold, call and raise. Within each group, the solver's own aggregate policy supplies the split. DCFR blends the fitted per-hand strategy with a flat target using the showdown strength coefficient, then blends that result with unlocked play using the confidence weight. The Phase 4H correction changes the first blend at the unopened SB root.
- Very-low-confidence reads have zero lock weight. Low, medium and high confidence begin at 5, 20 and 50 opportunities and use 0.30, 0.55 and 0.75 respectively. The regression checks both sides of each confidence boundary. Frequency smoothing adds one success and one failure, and groups are rounded to 0.05 with a positive floor. A weight limits each blend locally. It does not bound Hero's policy response, and repeated range conditioning can compound a weak assumption. A type label can still be unknown while one statistic starts to affect strategy.
- The four-hand prefix supplied two local flop betting and one facing-bet opportunity. Pooling made six and five respectively, so low-weight locks activated and moved the blocker-filtered range by 25.00 points and the mix by 8.94 points. Fold remained the modal and seeded action. The 12-hand prefix still had an unknown label and switched the mix by 72.27 points to a 99.41% call. Sparse evidence can produce a large adjustment under the current rules. This study supplies no independent calibration for how large that adjustment should be.
- Opportunity counts measure statistical support for the aggregated statistic. Some denominators do not match the node context. The preflop raise estimate pools all raise-legal decisions and the limp denominator includes unopened BB decisions. A single pooled postflop read can carry high confidence into a street with little local data, and the same response mix is used across wager sizes. Showdown coefficients share evidence across sizes, seats and streets, and are subject to selection because holdings observed at showdown survived earlier decisions. With no reveals in an action group the strength coefficient is 1 by prior, retaining solver-conditioned fitting. Frequent public actions can therefore produce a strong aggregate lock with little direct evidence for which hands took that line.
- The independent river references passed in all three arms. Holding the only royal flush at a 25.00% price produced equity 1 and call EV +750 chips. A board-wide royal at a 41.67% price produced equity 0.5 and call EV +250 chips. Fold was worth zero and the concrete recommendation was all-in to call in both spots. These exact values check terminal accounting under adaptation.
- A supplemental turn arithmetic check used a fixed card-blind opponent with an 18% fold rate to a shove and a uniform compatible range. Enumerating 46 river cards × 990 opponent holdings gave 76.1704% equity, yielding `0.18 × 750 + 0.82 × (0.761704 × 20,000 − 9,750) = +4,631.95` chips for shove and zero for fold. This confirms the direction of the shove against that reference relative to folding. Ranking the smaller raise and call requires a specified continuation through the river. The reference was selected after the matrix as an explanatory check and played no role in the benchmark trigger.
- Ordinary action EVs compare each arm against its own modeled continuation, with earlier-street approximation and no convergence certificate. The Coach labels the early-street approximation. Its action reason can say “best EV” within the modeled options, while the API supplies no measured exploitation gain. In particular, the Tight A8o shove and the loose-passive limp are real preflop decisions with no chart EV available to validate their strategic advantage.
- The exploitation toggle suppresses solver locks. The fallback still receives the original observation, including the profile used for display, and Expert can act on it. A separate forced-`ReplayError` probe replayed Jc 9d on Qh 7s 2d after a limp and two checks. With `exploit=False`, fallback advice checked without a profile and bet 100 with the Tight profile. Both responses reported `exploiting=false`. [Fallback probe](benchmark-results/phase-4i-2026-11-17/fallback-probe.json)

## Paired evaluation

The frozen gate required correction action TV of at least 5 points in two spots, or at least 5 points with a modal change in one. Zero spots qualified. The 24-pair panel stayed unrun. Phase 4I therefore has no new bb/100 estimate, paired interval or match-level fallback counts. The [Phase 4H panel](PHASE_4H_REPORT.md#paired-development-benchmark) remains the available fixed-read paired comparison. It reported 0.00 bb/100 correction delta and [0.00, 0.00] in 12 duplicate pairs at 100 bb with five affected root-lock exposures per arm. The separate retained 40-hand learning transcript did change under the correction. These results establish a possible policy effect elsewhere and give no estimate of its benefit in fresh learning sessions.

## Research direction

| Investment | Evidence and likely benefit | Implementation and compute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Improve range estimates | Could improve calibration where position, size and showdown selection distort reads. Large equity movements left several nearly pure choices intact, and historical size-conditioned locks reduced the paired pool score by 92.4 bb/100 [−123.4, −62.1]. [Historical size-conditioned trial](DESIGN_OPPONENT_MODEL.md#8-verdict-for-proposal-b-autopilot-step-20-applying-autopilotprereg-step-13md-mechanically) | Moderate modeling and validation effort with more context cells and thinner samples. Larger logged data requirements and potentially more profile-specific re-solves. |
| B. Improve how the live solver uses opponent ranges | Directly address the distinction between conditioned reach and a response-policy lock. Validate whether observed evidence changes the likelihood at the relevant public history, and whether the resulting choice improves payoff against the same independent reference opponent. | Moderate offline engineering. Start on terminal river branches with an exact payoff reference and the existing tree, so the comparison can run at current budgets. The cost of any extra online solve would be measured before integration. |
| C. Improve strategy or action abstraction | Could recover size-specific or later-street effects that the one-street tree cannot represent. A richer tree alone does not address unsupported assumptions, and the prior turn/river oracle study missed its practical-effect gate. [Stage 0 record](DESIGN_LATER_STREET_BETTING.md) | Highest complexity and runtime risk. More branches and future chance increase the cost of the dense range passes or require a different algorithm. |

**Recommend B.** Both policy entry points already have enough leverage to change decisions materially. The weakest link is establishing that the solver's action likelihoods and modeled responses at the actual public node are supported and improve the resulting choice. A more detailed range estimator would leave that question open, and a larger game tree would make the comparison more expensive.

## Proposed Phase 4J scope

Run an offline, river-first A/B study of posterior propagation and response locks. Freeze an independently defined weighted-combo opponent policy with known value/bluff splits, blocker interactions and firing/calling frequencies. Include distinct bet sizes, a no-observations control and small, medium and mature samples. Use the existing legal action menu and exact river payoffs against the same reference opponent in every arm, with marginal bluff-catchers on both sides of the pot-odds threshold.

Supply the exact conditional reach and response policy independently, then together, to measure the effect of each input. Compare the current replay path with one candidate that uses the observed-action likelihood for the actual decision context before solving the response. Hold chart, tree and training budgets fixed. Measure reference-opponent regret or EV, policy changes, range support, legality and runtime. Check the current “adjusted” indicator against the measured policy changes. Preregister the minimum practical EV effect, per-reference direction, sparse-evidence tolerance and runtime cap before examining a held-out panel. Gate any later gameplay test on passing those offline checks.
