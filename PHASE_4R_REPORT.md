# Phase 4R — Range-support integrity and solver uncertainty

The Phase 4Q fallback started with the turn solve's 0.5% relative-reach cutoff. It excluded 1,118 of Villain's 1,119 positive-weight holdings before evaluating the observed check. The check update carried forward only the solved columns. The river 6s legally removed the last holding, 6s 7s, and the river solver invoked the expert fallback before processing the shove. Full positive support repaired this replay in research. Its mean gain on the fresh panel was **+0.072 bb per decision**, with a **95% paired interval of [−0.0023, +0.2042] bb**. The candidate missed the interval and maximum replay-time gates. Production Coach and Match continue to use the incumbent solver.

## Live architecture checkpoint

| Stage | Verified behavior and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Coach input and profile | Coach validates card identity and street board length, and the hand engine enforces action order and legal target totals. The deck reveals only each street's prefix as it is reached. The advisor reads Hero's cards and public actions, bets, stacks and board. Optional adaptation rebuilds the profile from completed hands and the strength adjustment from public showdowns. The replay's Villain placeholder cards are invisible to the solver. |
| Preflop prior | Both public seat ranges start with unit weight on 1,326 combos. The nearest effective-stack chart at 15, 30, 60, 100 or 200 bb supplies action probabilities. The chart expands 169 classes onto individual holdings. A sufficiently supported profile can lock groups and re-solve the chart. The path multiplies the acting seat's reach once per observed action. Discrete depth and approximate target mapping affect reach even when replayed stacks and pot are exact. |
| Street reconstruction | The solver rebuilds the starting stacks from public contributions, reruns the path with those stacks, and checks the current street, actor, commitments and pot. It distinguishes a covering all-in call from an aggressive all-in. Board masks apply before solving each street, and pairwise compatibility accounts for cards shared between the two hypothetical holdings. Actual Hero blockers apply when conditioning on Hero's private cards, so public range columns can legally include Hero's cards as counterfactuals. |
| Computational pruning and action conditioning | Each solve retains positive weights greater than 0.005 times the board-legal maximum, plus the actual Hero column if needed. Each postflop action writes the actor's reach into a fresh zero vector using only those columns. Positive excluded holdings therefore become lasting zeros, even though their action likelihood was unevaluated. A flop directional bet shifted almost all of the failed hand's reach onto one combo. The cutoff erased a collectively substantial tail at the next solve. |
| River and off-menu sizes | River payoffs use exact board showdown ordering. Earlier streets use future-card equity at one-street leaves, with sampled flop runouts. Observed off-menu amounts map by log distance. Where the current street path deviates from the abstract tree, the solver re-solves at actual geometry using accumulated reach. It does not multiply the observed action twice. An earlier street path that leaves the abstract tree can still trigger an error. The displayed range can differ from the support used in a current-state re-solve. |
| Recommendation | Discounted CFR uses 60/70/80 iterations by street and a concrete size abstraction. The chosen action is sampled from the actual Hero column using a separate seeded RNG and legalized against the engine. The read-only EV pass uses modeled reach and replies, with EV relative to folding now. A finite, normalized strategy can coexist with very poor estimates. In particular, `action_values` returns zeros when compatible opponent reach is nonpositive; that value vector alone cannot distinguish an unsupported node from genuine indifference. |
| Uncertainty and fallback | Profile confidence controls lock strength, with bands beginning at 5, 20 and 50 opportunities. Pooled/opponent statistics and selected showdowns do not supply uncertainty intervals for each hand-by-action likelihood or for action EV. A conditional reach of one holding can produce a confident solver output. On caught replay/numeric errors the expert can still produce a legal action and the explanation contains a fallback reason. The frontend displays no strategy rows in that case. The production `empty range` reason does not identify the street or distinguish an observation with zero model reach from one hollowed out by pruning. |
| Match versus Coach | A Match configured with the adaptive solver shares the decision implementation. Match retains the bot, updates from completed hands and can reuse its per-hand street cache. Coach constructs an advisor for the requested spot and rebuilds its profile. Match may also use other registered policies, and its actual played histories follow sampled actions. A conditional Coach river comparison cannot establish Match win rate, learning over sessions, or resistance to opponent adaptation. |

## Failed replay

Deal 4, stream 131, tight-aggressive, three public hands. Hero 3c 5c on Qc 3s Ks 8h 6s, button, both starting at 1,500 with a 100-chip big blind. Hero opens to 250, Villain calls, Villain leads 250 on the flop, Hero calls, both check the turn, and Villain opens all-in for 1,000 into 1,000. After the river reveal and before the wager, both players have 1,000 behind and 500 committed from previous streets. The current river state after the wager has a 2,000 pot and 1,000 to cover. No further raise is possible.

The turn cutoff also discarded 31.67% of the mass conditional on Hero's cards. The retained turn check likelihood was 0.999978. The reported public raw masses are products of action probabilities and board masks, with an initial sum of 1,326. An action's mass reduction is likelihood weighting, whereas a solve cutoff discards weights before likelihood evaluation. Hero's replay had 374 positive public holdings and raw mass 0.000353233 when the river open solve failed. Every positive compatible snapshot normalizes to one within floating-point precision using its own compatible mass. The empty river snapshot has no normalized posterior. The full evidence records both seats, Hero and board exclusions, all remaining legal holdings at each step, explicit reveal removals, mapped actions and solve columns. **Root cause B, irreversible hard pruning.** The application of the 6s mask and the mapped geometry were correct. The check update multiplied the retained likelihood once. There was positive legal reach before the cutoff, and restoring computation for previously unevaluated positive holdings produced legal reach through the same public line. The evidence does not require an incompatible observation, a normalizer fault or a mask repair. The near-degenerate flop bet distribution made the cutoff dangerous. In the full-support solve the same bet still updates weights sharply, but the compatible effective size is 19.52 instead of 2.22. This implicates the interaction between computational support and coupled action inference. The exploration does not identify a correct real-opponent likelihood for the flop bet.

The three-hand profile activates preflop locks. The flop and turn solves in this hand have zero locks. With the profile, the estimated 250-chip flop bet probability for 6s 7s is 44.74%. Its post-bet weight is 0.347547 and the next-largest turn weight is 0.001496, below the 0.001738 cutoff. Replaying the same observations without the profile changes the estimated flop bet probability for that combo to 0.00129%, keeps 923 turn columns for the check and reaches the river normally. The sparse profile acts through the coupled preflop and flop ranges even though there is no direct lock on either postflop observation. Profile confidence therefore gives no bound on downstream concentration. [Profile ablation](benchmark-results/phase-4r-2026-11-18/profile-ablation.json)

## Research correction and range invariants

The candidate uses the same production replay and solve path with the relative cutoff set to zero. It computes likelihoods on every positive board-legal column, retains actual model zeros, and neither resurrects physically blocked cards nor inserts a likelihood floor. It validates legal posterior existence and uses an explicit research support error with the existing legal fallback for genuine empty reach. At a street root the inherited replay has already applied the board mask, so the stage and seat in that error identify where emptiness surfaced. The replay trace supplies the prior snapshot needed to determine what emptied it. The validator scales by the largest compatible weight before normalization, so a positive range whose raw weights are all subnormal can still be normalized. This validates conditional posterior arithmetic. The production CFR core still consumes raw reach and uses float32 terminal products. Arbitrarily long action sequences or extreme floating-point reach ratios need a separate scaled- or log-reach design and solver-level numerical controls.

The known replay retains 1,128 positive public combinations after Villain's turn check, 1,081 after 6s, and 990 compatible positive holdings after the shove. Effective compatible size is 128.98 after the shove. With the same independent Phase 4Q reference, fold earns 0 and covering loses 3.863 bb at 20.46% true equity. The incumbent fallback folds. The research recommendation also folds, with 99.9876% fold probability and −0.00048 bb exact EV for the full mix. Fixing the missing solve here confers a reliability benefit and leaves the modal decision unchanged. The candidate still estimates just 10.16% equity, which distinguishes support integrity from accuracy.

Independent controls enumerate combinations from the remaining deck at each street, check blocker removal of the highest-weight hand, preserve known subnormal odds on surviving legal hands, reject invalid weights and distinguish zero likelihood from a blocking mask that removes positive reach. All five action kinds exercise the same multiplicative update with a hand-computed posterior and contradictory observation. The actual replay includes check, call, bet, preflop raise and shove, while paired holdout comparisons also exercise three-bet paths. Invalid or repeated cards are rejected by Coach before advice. ## Decision uncertainty and historical extremes

For a closing wager with cost C and final contested pot T, call EV is T times equity minus C. Moving a posterior by total variation ε changes equity by at most ε and cover EV by at most Tε, because each holding's outcome lies in [0,1]. A call near break-even can therefore flip under a modest reweighting. A tight confidence margin around a seriously biased posterior can still favor an expensive mistake. Effective size measures concentration, and a broad range can be biased in aggregate.

| Archived decision | Bad modeled equity / break-even | True equity | Exact EV of cover | Fixed-prior wager-oracle equity |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Js 3c on 2d Ah Jh 2h Kh, 2,400 into 1,200 | 41.76% / 40.00% | 8.80% | −18.720 bb | 8.80% |
| Kc As on Th 8s 4h Qs 7s, 2,500 into 1,000 | 54.93% / 41.67% | 0.47% | −24.716 bb | 8.51% |

The **18.72 bb** historical error requires the exact incoming prior in the counterfactual arm and the incumbent wager likelihood. Replacing only the wager likelihood repairs the ranking. Mixing 5% toward that oracle moves posterior TV by 0.0217 and leaves estimated cover EV at +0.067 bb. Mixing 10% moves TV by 0.0434 and pushes it to −0.922 bb. The live baseline with its own incoming prior already favors folding, with exact mixed EV −0.04365 bb, and support-only replay scores −0.04260 bb. The likelihood error is large relative to the truth, while the modeled ranking is close to its threshold.

The **−24.715 bb** adjustment in Phase 4Q is the difference between two near-deterministic fixed-value policies. It is driven by the Phase 4Q likelihood change on an unchanged production prior for the tight-passive reference. At 25% movement toward the wager oracle (TV 0.147), modeled cover EV is still +0.995 bb. At 50% (TV 0.293), it is −5.969 bb. This requires a much larger change than in the other historical case. The actual incumbent live mix gives 31.95% to the cover even with a negative final modeled value, and scores −7.897 bb. The support-only live mix gives 30.45% to the cover and scores −7.525 bb despite going from 213 to 990 legal positive holdings. Both are distinct from the archived likelihood experiment's policy. Closing coverage has no future response branch, so response probabilities cannot explain either closing loss. Under the aggressive references in Phase 4Q, an accurate wager law can still misrank the cover when the incoming range is wrong. [Historical perturbations and actual mixed policies](benchmark-results/phase-4r-2026-11-18/historical.json) · [Phase 4Q details](PHASE_4Q_REPORT.md)

The distinction between displayed final action EV and an actual mixed strategy matters. The live policy is an iteration-averaged strategy of the coupled game, not the argmax of final modeled action values. The Kc As case shows how residual mixing can incur substantial exact regret. Phase 4L showed the opposite failure mode when a greedy selector committed to a mispriced shove. For nonclosing wagers, its historical Kd Td case showed how an overstated further-raise opportunity inflated a small bet's modeled EV. More decisive selection alone can amplify whichever prior, likelihood or continuation error dominates the spot. [Phase 4L](PHASE_4L_REPORT.md)

## Architecture decision and roadmap

| Option | Cost and feasibility | Expected benefit and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep the chart and repair inference | Low-to-moderate scope. Preserve existing chart and tree fixtures. Separate persistent positive reach from temporary solve columns, instrument omitted mass and empty conditional support, and validate amount/actor/card semantics. | Directly addresses the reproducible failure. The 9 bb panel exercises a nearest-chart extrapolation. Accurate stack-aware prior weights and off-menu likelihoods remain independent research questions. |
| Retain one-street DCFR with a separate decision-risk evaluation | Moderate scope. A closing-wager audit is exact and local. Nonclosing uncertainty requires bounded continuation probes and comparable concrete menus. | Can expose brittle decisions and separate input error from strategy averaging. Earlier-street equity leaves and lock assumptions still limit strategic quality. |
| Improve action-size modeling where the oracle measures regret | Moderate scope if targeted. Log offered, mapped and legal targets and compare omitted-versus-supported optima. Prototype off-tree size handling against explicit references. | Phase 4L found a mean menu loss of 0.00487 bb on its panel, below modeled range/response regret there. Improving size mapping may still matter on other lines, especially when it changes reach or reopening. |
| Larger MCCFR training or a replacement solver | Highest offline and maintenance cost, including checkpoint convergence, chance abstractions, action translation and online latency. Keep bounded exact games and current replay as independent controls. | More training may reduce approximation error inside a correctly specified game. It cannot repair erased legal reach, selected observations, an incorrect hand-by-size law or a mispriced reply model. A learned blueprint with constrained online re-solving is worth a small offline comparison if targeted reliability and input repairs plateau under fair gameplay tests. |

The accumulated evidence supports targeted repair. Phase 4H corrected an independently verified opening-lock classification. Phase 4I demonstrated decision sensitivity without a playing-strength result. Phase 4J found a disabled-fallback profile leak and positive conditional EV for adaptation, while its confidence limiter missed promotion. Phase 4K's exact gain did not translate to its paired gameplay gate. Phase 4L isolated both range and response error and rejected greedy conversion. Phase 4M's response refinement improved marginal Brier while exact EV fell 0.051 bb in the primary panel and 0.044 bb against raising stress references. Phase 4N's posterior refinement gained +0.197 bb but missed the interval and downside gates. Phase 4O restored missing support with near-zero mean EV change in its fixed-input design. Phase 4P's prior change missed uncertainty and downside gates. Phase 4Q's likelihood gain accompanied weaker decisions, a severe closing error and an incumbent integrity failure. These results justify keeping the architecture as a testable baseline for competitive research. Competitive adaptive play itself remains unproven. [4H](PHASE_4H_REPORT.md) · [4I](PHASE_4I_REPORT.md) · [4J](PHASE_4J_REPORT.md) · [4K](PHASE_4K_REPORT.md) · [4L](PHASE_4L_REPORT.md) · [4M](PHASE_4M_REPORT.md) · [4N](PHASE_4N_REPORT.md) · [4O](PHASE_4O_REPORT.md) · [4P](PHASE_4P_REPORT.md) · [4Q](PHASE_4Q_REPORT.md)

1. Establish a typed replay/support contract, with legal support, mass excluded, source of each zero, compatibility and current-state geometry visible to offline evaluation. Verify the observer's all-in-call classification against public history as an independent correctness correction.
2. Decouple persisted reach from solver columns. Benchmark a sparse strategy against the full-support control, representing positive tail likelihood explicitly and preserving genuine zeros. Measure omitted reach after subsequent blocker masks, not just its predicted size at solve time.
3. Quantify decision uncertainty before intensifying exploitation. Test closing equity and EV intervals first and measure exact regret and worst losses. Expand to a separate continuation uncertainty interface only with a trustworthy estimate of replies from existing public or controlled data.
4. Require independent exact-EV gates before attempting duplicate-deal gameplay across depth, profile sparsity, varied sizing and opponent mismatch. Include nonadaptive and adaptive policies as comparators and record legality, fallback frequency, latency and memory. Compare a stronger bounded offline solver only after diagnosing residual abstraction or convergence regret.

**Proposed Phase 4S.** Preregister a research-only persistent reach and solve-support interface. Use direct sparse-plus-tail posterior arithmetic with explicit legal normalization, and compare it to the Phase 4R full-column control with fixed size menus and solver work limits. Require exact support guarantees and zero unexpected fallbacks on adversarial blocker and genuine contradiction fixtures and a fresh independently generated replay panel. Where a genuine model contradiction warrants an explicit refusal to advise, register that status and how it counts before opening the panel. Then test a single closing-wager decision rule against preregistered perturbation radii and independent exact EV. Use separate strata for inherent posterior concentration and large unexplained likelihood error. Stable EV and cost bounds are prerequisites for gameplay validation.
